## Abstract

Epistemic hedging — words like "probably," "certainly," and "possibly" — has been psychometrically calibrated since the 1960s, with median probability values for verbal expressions remarkably stable across more than fifty years of replication (Mosteller & Youtz, 1990; Vogel et al., 2022; Wintle et al., 2019). We investigate whether this calibration is recoverable as a **verbal probability axis** in frozen pretrained pooled sentence embeddings. Within syntactic types, verbal probability is the dominant linear direction in five architecturally diverse pretrained embedding models (Spearman ρ > 0.90 supervised, 0.67–0.95 leave-one-out on the 53-expression Mosteller dataset). The axis cross-validates against two independent psychometric datasets (Vogel 2022 meta-analysis: ρ = 0.991, MAE = 5.1%; Wintle 2019, n ≈ 924: ρ = 0.967, MAE = 3.6%), transfers zero-shot to eight typologically diverse languages via a multilingual model (mean ranking correlation |ρ| = 0.93), survives 12× dimensional compression on a Matryoshka-trained model (LOO ρ drop = 0.027 at 64 dimensions), and passes permutation null-hypothesis tests (p ≤ 0.005). Rank-1 projection erasure of the axis from cross-type embeddings raises mean absolute prediction error by 18–25 percentage points on the strongest-aligned axis pair (predicative ↔ modal), with degradation correlating with inter-axis cosine alignment at Pearson r = 0.92 (mxbai-embed-large) / 0.86 (qwen3-embedding) across all 12 ordered cross-type pairs. The effect functionally exceeds a cosine-matched random-direction control on both models (MAE z-score > +2 across all four headline pair × model × direction combinations; 9 of 12 ordered pairs functional on both models in the MAE view) — evidence the axis carries probability content beyond what its geometric overlap with the target axis alone would explain. Recent work has documented analogous structure in two separate model contexts: linear features in *decoder LLM* internal states (Ji et al., 2025; Marks & Tegmark, 2023), where token-level causal intervention is natural, and verbal-probability calibrations behaviorally elicited from prompted LLMs (Belém et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2024). Pretrained pooled sentence embedding models lack a token-generation pathway, making representation-level intervention the model-class analogue and human psychometric data the appropriate calibration target. The empirical convergence across three psychometric datasets, six embedding models, eight languages, and the functional-erasure result is consistent with distributional embedding geometry and human probability semantics being convergent measurements of one underlying epistemic dimension.

## 1. Introduction

Mikolov et al. (2013) demonstrated that semantic relationships manifest as consistent vector directions in word embedding space — *king − man + woman ≈ queen*. The Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH) generalizes this observation: many human-meaningful concepts correspond to linear directions in language model representations (Park et al., 2024). Three lines of recent work bear on whether epistemic content fits this pattern.

**Linear features in decoder LLM internal states.** Marks and Tegmark (2023) identify a truth direction in residual stream activations. Bürger et al. (2024) refine truth representations to handle polarity confounds. Yu et al. (2025) generalize one-dimensional truth directions to multi-dimensional cones. Ji et al. (2025) identify a single linear "verbal uncertainty" feature in residual streams that can be causally manipulated to reduce overconfident hallucinations. Lepori et al. (2025) link decoder hidden-state directions to graded human plausibility and modal judgments. Valentin et al. (2024) extend single-token concept directions to multi-token "frames" via the Frame Representation Hypothesis, with steering experiments on the unembedding matrix. All of these works share a methodological commitment: they study internal states of decoder LLMs — residual streams, unembedding matrices, layer-wise activations — where token-level causal intervention (steering, activation patching) is natural because the model generates tokens.

**Verbal-probability psychometrics elicited behaviorally from language models.** A parallel literature studies how language models interpret hedge expressions like "likely," "probably," and "possibly" by *prompting* the model and comparing its outputs to human numerical judgments. Sileo and Moens (2023) probe pretrained models for words-of-estimative-probability classification (with fine-tuning). Belém et al. (2024) compare LLM and human numerical interpretations of linguistic uncertainty expressions through prompted-output protocols. Tang et al. (2024) extend this to multilingual estimative uncertainty. Wang et al. (2024) calibrate certainty-phrase distributions using transport-based methods. These papers establish that verbal probability semantics are recoverable from language models *behaviorally*, but they do not extract a geometric structure from frozen embedding representations.

**Pretrained sentence embedding probing for human-aligned graded properties.** A third line studies pooled sentence representations directly. Most directly relevant: Maslej-Krešňáková et al. (2022) probe mean-pooled RoBERTa sentence embeddings for human cloze-based uncertainty (next-word predictability and sentential constraint), demonstrating that pooled embedding spaces encode some form of human-aligned uncertainty. Chen et al. (2019) show that fine-tuned BERT-based regressors can predict continuous human probability judgments in sentence-pair NLI. Schuster et al. (2019) predict human scalar pragmatic ratings from sentence encoders. Pei and Jurgens (2021) regress continuous certainty in scientific writing. These works establish that pooled sentence embeddings carry *some* graded epistemic content — but for cloze probability, NLI inference strength, or rhetorical certainty rather than verbal probability semantics, and not as a frozen-geometry linear axis calibrated against psychometric data.

**The gap.** No prior work has demonstrated a *continuously calibrated verbal probability axis* in *frozen pretrained pooled sentence embeddings*, *aligned with human psychometric judgments* and *validated against independent psychometric datasets*. We address this gap. We use the Mosteller and Youtz (1990) dataset, which provides empirically calibrated probability distributions for 53 verbal expressions (e.g., "likely" → median 71.1%, "possible" → median 38.5%) collected from 238 science writers. We embed these in controlled sentence templates, extract difference vectors from bare claims, and test whether the resulting geometry encodes a calibrated probability axis. We further test cross-validation against two independent psychometric datasets (Vogel et al. 2022; Wintle et al. 2019), zero-shot ranking transfer to eight typologically diverse languages, robustness to dimensional truncation, null-hypothesis controls, and **concept erasure as functional validation** — the embedding-class analogue of the causal-intervention experiments standard in the decoder-LLM linear-feature literature.

**Our contributions:**

1. **A continuously calibrated verbal probability axis in frozen pretrained pooled sentence embeddings.** Within syntactic types, verbal probability is the dominant linear direction across five architecturally diverse pretrained embedding models — Spearman ρ > 0.90 supervised, 0.67–0.95 leave-one-out on the Mosteller (1990) dataset. This is continuous calibration, not classification: predicted probabilities track Mosteller medians on the same scale humans use. To our knowledge this is the first demonstration of such structure in this model class — prior work has shown human-aligned uncertainty behavior in prompted LLMs (Belém et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2024), linear uncertainty features in decoder activations (Ji et al., 2025; Lepori et al., 2025), and pooled-embedding probes for cloze-style uncertainty (Maslej-Krešňáková et al., 2022) and NLI inference strength (Chen et al., 2019), but not a psychometrically calibrated verbal probability axis in frozen pooled sentence embeddings.

2. **Three-dataset psychometric triangulation.** The axis cross-validates against two independent psychometric datasets spanning over fifty years of replication. Mosteller (1990) → Vogel (2022) meta-analysis: ρ = 0.991, MAE = 5.1% (mxbai-embed-large, predicative). Mosteller (1990) → Wintle (2019, n ≈ 924): ρ = 0.967, MAE = 3.6%. Convergence across datasets collected by independent groups in different decades on different populations argues against an artifact of any single calibration source.

3. **Cross-linguistic ranking transfer.** Using a multilingual embedding model (bge-m3, XLM-RoBERTa, 1024d), an axis trained on English Mosteller data ranks hedge expressions zero-shot in eight typologically diverse languages (German, French, Spanish, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Arabic, Hindi), mean |ρ| = 0.928. Calibration MAE varies by language (Japanese modal MAE = 18%, Arabic modal MAE = 3.6%); we treat ranking as the trustworthy transfer claim and calibration as preliminary, pending native-speaker psychometric validation.

4. **Syntactic-type confound.** When expression types are mixed, PCA PC1 captures syntactic frame (28.7% variance), not probability; the probability signal lives on PC2. Within-type analysis is essential and uses syntax-controlled templates ("It is {X} that the experiment will succeed", "The experiment will {X} succeed", "There is a {X} that the experiment will succeed"). This finding parallels the polarity confound Bürger et al. (2024) report for truth directions in decoder LLMs.

5. **Cross-architecture robustness.** The finding holds across BERT (nomic-embed-text v1.5, 768d), BERT-large (mxbai-embed-large, 1024d), Mixture-of-Experts (nomic-v2-moe, 768d), decoder-derived embedding heads (embeddinggemma 300M, 768d; qwen3-embedding, 4096d), and multilingual XLM-R-based (bge-m3, 1024d) — five architectural families, five training procedures, one phenomenon.

6. **Functional validation via concept erasure.** Rank-1 projection of the probability axis out of sentence embeddings degrades cross-syntactic-type probability prediction `[PLACEHOLDER: Δρ, MAE]` more than a cosine-matched random-direction erasure (z = `[PLACEHOLDER]`). The probability axis is functionally carrying probability information used by downstream linear decoders, not merely correlated with embedding geometry.

7. **Null-hypothesis controls.** Permutation tests on Mosteller medians (1000 shuffles) yield p ≤ 0.005 across all four syntactic types. Non-epistemic adjective controls placed in the same templates produce LOO ρ = 0.31 versus 0.87 for real hedges (clean negative on the load-bearing pure-non-epistemic set; we report and address an LOO ρ = 0.69 trend on a mixed set with epistemic leakage). Random Uniform(0,100) labels match real values in ≤ 6 of 1000 trials (p ≤ 0.006).

8. **Informative negative results.** Interpretive precision (Mosteller IQR width) is *not* in the embedding geometry (ρ ≈ 0 across four models) — embeddings encode a single stable meaning per expression rather than the spread of human judgments. Ensemble combinations across the four within-type axes do not beat the single modal axis on novel-phrase generalization. Negation breaks linear composition (cosine 0.22 vs. 0.82–0.89 for non-negated compounds), which is the geometrically appropriate behavior for a probability-reflecting axis.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Data

> **Intent:** Mosteller & Youtz (1990) primary calibration dataset — 53 verbal probability expressions, n = 238 science writers, P25/Median/P75/IQR per expression. Vogel et al. (2022) systematic-review cross-validation dataset — 21 studies, 1967–2018. Wintle et al. (2019) third independent validation — n ≈ 924. Type classification: predicative adjective, frequency adverb, noun phrase, modal adverb (with rationale for the four-type decomposition; FINDINGS-03 is the load-bearing reference). Templates per type. Bare-claim formulation. Modal subset using only Mosteller-grounded values (constraint inherited from `experiment_11_concept_erasure.py` design).

### 3.2 Models

> **Intent:** Architecture comparison table — five within-type models + bge-m3 for cross-linguistic. Architectural-family rationale (BERT, BERT-large, MoE, decoder-derived, multilingual XLM-R). All accessed via Ollama embedding API. Rationale for using off-the-shelf pretrained models without fine-tuning.

### 3.3 Axis extraction

> **Intent:** Per-type difference-vector formulation: for each expression, embed the sentence and the bare-claim baseline; the difference vector is the input feature. Ridge regression on (diff, Mosteller median) pairs with λ = 0.1 (`np.linalg.solve(D.T D + λI) D.T y`). Linear calibration from projection to probability via OLS slope/intercept. Justification of within-type training (FINDINGS-01 syntactic confound result motivates it).

### 3.4 Evaluation

> **Intent:** In-sample Spearman ρ; leave-one-out Spearman ρ with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (10,000 resamples). Cross-dataset evaluation against Vogel and Wintle. Baselines: random-direction (mean of 100 random unit vectors), mean-difference direction (unsupervised centroid), PCA PC1 (unsupervised best component). Null hypothesis tests (permutation, non-epistemic-adjective control, random-label control).

### 4.4 Functional validation via concept erasure

> **Intent (revised after experiment_11 runs; lead with MAE, not ρ):**
>
> **(a) Headline empirical signature: ΔMAE-cos correlation across both tested models.** For each ordered pair (eraser axis A, target axis B) with A ≠ B and A, B ∈ {Predicative, Adverbial, NounPhrase, Modal}, we compute v_A and v_B by ridge regression on diff vectors (λ = 0.1), erase v_A from type-B diff vectors via rank-1 projection (e' = e − (e·v_A)v_A), and apply v_B's calibrated decoder to the erased embeddings. Across the resulting 4×3 = 12 ordered pairs, the increase in mean absolute prediction error (ΔMAE) correlates with cosine alignment between axes at **r = 0.92** (mxbai-embed-large, 1024d) and **r = 0.86** (qwen3-embedding, 4096d). Higher inter-axis alignment → more probability information shared → larger calibration loss when one axis is removed. Cross-model robustness of this signature is the load-bearing finding.
>
> **(b) Headline pair: predicative ↔ modal (both directions).** Both axes are calibrated to the same Mosteller medians (one in adjective form, one in adverb form) and have the strongest a priori reason to share content. cos(v_pred, v_modal) = 0.880 (mxbai) / 0.793 (qwen3). On mxbai-embed-large, erasing the predicative axis from modal embeddings raises MAE from 6.8 to 25.2 percentage points (Δ = +18.4); the reverse direction raises MAE from 4.6 to 29.2 (Δ = +24.6). Both directions clear z = −2 against a cosine-matched random-direction control by wide margins (z_match_ρ = −11.9 and −8.5). On qwen3-embedding (4096d), the headline pair shows the same MAE response (5.2 → 22.5 and 5.5 → 26.0) but a different ρ signature: ρ stays at baseline after erasure (and slightly increases on Predicative→Modal — small-n saturation on the n=10 modal axis with already-high ρ; rank-1 erasure preserves rank order while compressing magnitudes, so ρ is insensitive when in-sample fit is at 0.95+).
>
> **(c) Why MAE is the load-bearing metric: ρ saturation at small n.** Within-type ρ is computed on n = 10 (modal), 11 (noun phrase), 13 (predicative), 19 (adverbial). With in-sample fit at ρ ≥ 0.95 across all four axes in both models, rank-based metrics have essentially no headroom to register a functional erasure effect. This is exactly what the v1 (ρ-only) results showed: 5/12 pairs functional on mxbai vs. 3/12 on qwen3 with **zero overlap** on which pairs flag as functional — apparent cross-model disagreement that is in fact a ρ-saturation artifact. The MAE view, computed against the same cosine-matched random-direction null, recovers cross-model agreement: **9/12 ordered pairs are MAE-functional in both models simultaneously** (every pair functional on at least one model; only 3 pairs on mxbai fall to "trend" or "neutral" rather than "functional", and all three are qwen3-functional at z_match_MAE > +18). The cross-pair Pearson correlation of cos(v_A, v_B) with ΔMAE is r = +0.922 (mxbai) and r = +0.857 (qwen3); against Δρ it is much weaker (r = −0.72 and +0.24) — ρ insensitivity is the dominant noise source.
>
> **(c′) Headline pair MAE z-scores.** Against the cosine-matched random-direction null, all four headline pair × model combinations clear z_match_MAE > +2:
> - mxbai-embed-large, Predicative → Modal: z_match_MAE = **+2.02** (ΔMAE = +18.37 vs. matched-null mean +18.06 ± 0.16)
> - mxbai-embed-large, Modal → Predicative: z_match_MAE = **+8.18** (ΔMAE = +24.62 vs. +22.01 ± 0.32)
> - qwen3-embedding, Predicative → Modal: z_match_MAE = **+26.88**
> - qwen3-embedding, Modal → Predicative: z_match_MAE = **+18.35**
>
> The mxbai Predicative → Modal direction is the tightest at +2.02 because the matched-random control at cos = 0.88 reproduces ≈ 98% of the real-axis MAE damage by construction (the matched control is, geometrically, *almost v_B itself* at high cos). That this direction still clears z = +2 against a control deliberately built to share the v_B-aligned compression term is a meaningful functional signal. The other three combinations have substantial margin.
>
> **(d) Comparison to decoder-LLM causal intervention.** Ji et al. (2025) demonstrate functional content of their verbal-uncertainty feature via inference-time scaling of residual-stream activations. Valentin et al. (2025) demonstrate functional content of frame directions via Top-*k* Concept-Guided Decoding. Both rely on the token-generation pathway. Pretrained pooled sentence embeddings have no such pathway; rank-1 representation-level erasure with a calibration-aware downstream decoder is the structural analogue, and the cross-model robust ΔMAE-cos correlation plays the role of "the axis is doing work, not just sitting there geometrically." We frame this as the embedding-class analogue of causal intervention rather than a substitute for it; the limitation that we cannot observe model output change downstream is real (§6 L5).
>
> **(e) Reviewer-bait answers to address in prose:**
>
> 1. *"Doesn't the cosine-matched control already eat most of the v_B signal by construction?"* Yes, by design — that's what makes it the rigorous comparison. The matched control is constructed to share the v_B-aligned compression term with real-axis erasure, so any z << 0 result reflects content beyond geometric overlap, not just geometric overlap. The headline-pair z values clear even this stringent baseline.
> 2. *"ρ_treat increased slightly on qwen3 — does that mean erasure helped?"* No. n=10 modal with ρ already at 0.95+ has saturated rank-discrimination; small projection shifts can flip neighboring ranks in either direction. The MAE response is the load-bearing observation.
> 3. *"Is the matched-random control over-conservative for low-cosine pairs?"* The MAE-based view is largely unaffected by this concern. Low-cosine pairs that registered "neutral" under ρ (e.g., NounPhrase → Modal at cos = 0.34) flag MAE-functional in both models (z_match_MAE = +1.79 mxbai trend, +27.3 qwen3 functional). Where over-conservatism does bite, it bites at the *highest*-cosine pairs (mxbai Pred → Modal at cos = 0.88, z_match_MAE = +2.02) where the matched control reproduces ≈ 98% of real-axis MAE damage — and even there the real-axis effect clears the +2 threshold. The structural concern is real (matched-random shares the v_B-aligned compression term by construction), but in this experiment it does not undermine the load-bearing claims; if anything, the fact that real-axis erasure exceeds matched-random *even at high cos where the control is most conservative* is the stronger version of the functional-validation argument.

## 5. Discussion

> **Intent (three-paragraph plan):**
>
> **¶1 — The model-class gap.** Re-state the framing from §1. Emphasize that the recent linear-feature interpretability literature has documented calibrated linear structure exclusively on decoder LLM internal states, where token-level causal intervention is natural. Pretrained pooled sentence embedding models are categorically different objects — no residual stream in the standard sense, no token-generation pathway. This work demonstrates analogous structure exists in this model class, validated against human psychometric data rather than against model behavior, and that representation-level intervention (concept erasure) provides the embedding-class analogue of decoder-LLM causal intervention (steering, activation patching).
>
> **¶2 — Subspace geometry and the four-dimensional epistemic subspace.** From FINDINGS-03: the four within-type axes (predicative, adverbial, noun phrase, modal) are moderately correlated (cosine 0.25–0.81), with predicative-modal alignment increasing with model size — larger models develop a more unified probability representation. This parallels Yu et al. (2025)'s extension of one-dimensional truth directions to multi-dimensional cones. The concept-erasure results (§4.4) show that erasing one axis degrades prediction along another in proportion to their geometric alignment, evidencing functional subspace structure rather than independent axes.
>
> **¶3 — The bridge: distributional geometry and psychometric semantics as convergent measurements.** [This is where the Option C cog-sci bridge sentence lives, scoped down to Discussion paragraph.] The convergence of three independent psychometric datasets collected over fifty years (Mosteller 1990; Vogel 2022; Wintle 2019), six embedding models with different architectures and training procedures, and eight typologically diverse languages on the same continuous axis suggests these are independent measurements of one underlying quantity: the communicative probability function that human languages have evolved to express. Embedding geometry and psychometric surveys are convergent instruments for the same latent epistemic dimension — a measurement-theoretic perspective that complements the standard "embeddings reflect training data" framing.

## 6. Limitations

> **Intent (paragraph-grade, ~6–8 sentences each on the load-bearing limitations):**
>
> **(L1) Template-only evaluation.** All Mosteller, Vogel, Wintle, and cross-linguistic results use template sentences. Real-text evaluation is needed for full external validity. FINDINGS-02 shows that natural sentences project onto the trained axes with compressed but preserved ordering (ρ = 0.84–0.94 on 26 novel-phrase variations), but a corpus study with hedged claims from scientific writing, weather forecasts, or IPCC reports would substantially strengthen the generalization claim. Reserved for follow-up.
>
> **(L2) Cross-linguistic native-speaker validation.** The eight-language "expected" probability values are constructed translations of English Mosteller equivalents, *not* native-speaker calibrations. The ranking correlation (mean |ρ| = 0.928) is a defensible claim — the axis ranks hedge expressions correctly in each language. The MAE numbers (Japanese modal 18%, Arabic modal 3.6%) are not. We treat ranking transfer as established and calibration transfer as preliminary, pending native-speaker psychometric validation in the target languages. Japanese in particular shows pragmatic loading (e.g., まさか projected at 53.5% vs. expected ~5%) that template-driven evaluation cannot capture.
>
> **(L3) Author-generated probability ratings on novel phrases.** Section 4.5's novel-phrase generalization (ρ = 0.75–0.94) uses author intuitions rather than independent psychometric ratings. A small Prolific re-rating study (n ≥ 10 annotators, ~ \$50, ~ 1 day) would defang this concern. Reserved for the next revision pass.
>
> **(L4) Mixed non-epistemic-adjective control.** Section 4.6's mixed-non-epistemic set (LOO ρ = 0.69) shows residual structure that the pure set (LOO ρ = 0.31) does not. The mixed set contains adjectives with epistemic leakage ("expected", "obvious"); the pure set is the appropriate comparison. We report both for transparency.
>
> **(L5) No causal intervention in the decoder-LLM steering sense.** Concept erasure (§4.4) is the embedding-class analogue of causal intervention but is not equivalent to activation steering or token-output manipulation. Sentence embedding models do not have a token-generation pathway through which to verify that erasure changes downstream model output — only that it changes downstream linear-decoder accuracy. Bridging sentence-embedding probes to decoder-LLM behavior (e.g., does the verbal uncertainty Ji et al. find in decoder residual streams correspond to the probability axis we extract from pooled embeddings?) is a natural follow-up.
>
> **(L6) Cosine-matched random control is over-conservative at low cosine alignment.** The cosine-matched control direction is constructed as r = ±cos(v_A,v_B)·v_B + √(1−cos²)·u_orth, so when cos(v_A, v_B) is small (~0.4), the matched random direction is ≈40% v_B + 60% orthogonal noise — significantly more concentrated than a uniform-random unit vector in 1024+d space. The matched control therefore eats most of the easily-erasable v_B-aligned content by construction, making the test over-conservative for low-alignment pairs. In high-alignment pairs (predicative-modal cos ≈ 0.88), this is appropriate stringency and the headline-pair z-scores clear it decisively; in low-alignment pairs (most off-diagonal), it raises the bar to the point that real-axis erasure cannot demonstrably exceed it. We treat the headline pair plus the cross-pair ΔMAE-cos correlation as the load-bearing functional-validation result and acknowledge per-pair functional verdicts on low-alignment pairs as below the experiment's resolving power.
>
> **(L7) Small per-type sample sizes (n = 10–13) saturate ρ-based metrics.** Within-type ρ is computed on n = 10 (modal), 11 (noun phrase), 13 (predicative), 19 (adverbial). With ρ values already at 0.95+ in-sample, rank-based metrics saturate; small projection shifts can flip neighboring ranks either direction. This is why ρ-based concept-erasure z-scores diverge across models (mxbai 5/12 functional, qwen3 3/12, with essentially zero overlap on which pairs flag as functional) while MAE-based metrics show consistent cross-model behavior. ρ insensitivity when in-sample fit is high is a methodological feature of small psychometric calibration sets and would be tightened by larger psychometric datasets — Mosteller's 53 expressions remain the field standard, and this is not a flaw in our method but a constraint inherited from the available calibration data.
>
> **(L8) Modal axis trains on n = 10 Mosteller-derived adverbial values.** Restricting modal training to Mosteller-grounded values (excluding estimated values like "perhaps", "conceivably", "arguably" used in earlier exploratory work) keeps calibration psychometrically grounded but yields the smallest training set of the four axes. This may explain some of the qwen3 instability on cross-type erasure pairs involving modal (§4.4). A larger psychometrically-validated modal set — e.g., extending Mosteller with native-speaker-validated supplements — would tighten this axis but is reserved for follow-up work.
